A business sends a supplier 30 USDC. On most chains, that payment is now public forever: the counterparties, the amount, and enough history to reconstruct the margin.

For a consumer buying coffee, that might not matter. For a business, it is a leak. Competitors can see your suppliers, your prices, your volumes, and your cash position. Payroll becomes a public document. The thing that makes a public blockchain trustworthy, that every transaction is visible and verifiable, is the same thing that makes it unusable for ordinary commercial payments.

Privacy systems exist to fix exactly this. RAILGUN is one of the strongest: a privacy layer that lives inside a smart contract on a normal chain, so you keep the chain's settlement and liquidity while hiding the details of each payment. The problem has never really been the cryptography. The problem is that the cryptography is brutal to use.

To send one private payment by hand, a person normally has to install a wallet extension, write down a seed phrase, buy a gas token, bridge funds to the right chain, run a slow sync of the private pool, shield funds manually, wait for a compliance check, find the recipient's long private address, generate a proof in the browser, and sign. Every one of those is a place to get stuck, overpay, or lose money. A normal business owner stops at step two.

Monaris is a bet that this entire wall can be removed without removing the privacy. The person signs in with an email. The same private payment becomes a few taps. The hard parts still happen, but they happen invisibly, inside the browser, where the user never has to see them.

The cryptography is the same on both sides. What changes is who carries the complexity.

01 / THE FOUNDATION

What RAILGUN actually is

Most people picture a crypto balance as a number in an account, like a bank balance that goes up and down. RAILGUN does not work that way, and the difference is the key to everything else.

In RAILGUN, value lives as a set of discrete notes, the way cash lives as a set of bills. If you hold a 50 and two 20s, you do not have a "90 balance," you have three separate notes that happen to add up to 90. Your private balance is simply the sum of the notes you own. This is the same model that Bitcoin and Zcash use, and it matters because notes can be spent, split, and recombined without ever exposing a running account total.

All of these notes live together in one place: a shielded pool inside a smart contract. Everyone's notes are mixed in the same pool. On chain, a note is not a name or an amount. It is just a hash, sitting next to hundreds of other hashes.

Shielding is public. Privacy does not come from hiding the deposit. It comes from the crowd you join afterward.

That last point is worth stating plainly, because it is the most common misunderstanding. Putting money into the pool is public. Anyone can see that your address shielded 100 USDC. The privacy is not in the deposit. It comes later, from the fact that your note is now mixed with everyone else's, and a future spend cannot be linked back to the money you originally put in. The bigger the pool, the stronger the privacy.

RAILGUN in plain english

Note (commitment)  A unit of private value, like a single bill. On chain it appears only as a hash, called a commitment. It hides the owner, the amount, and the token.

Shield / unshield  Moving public funds into the pool (shield) or back out to a public address (unshield). Both are visible. Everything in between is not.

Merkle root  A single number that is a fingerprint of every note ever added to the pool. If even one note changes, the root changes.

Nullifier  A one-time tag published when a note is spent. It proves "this note is now used" without revealing which note it was, which is how double-spending is blocked.

0zk address  A private receiving address inside RAILGUN. Paying it reveals nothing about the recipient on the public chain.

Proof of Innocence  A compliance gate. Before a note can be spent, aggregator nodes check its history against lists of stolen or sanctioned funds, so the pool cannot quietly become a laundering tool.

02 / THE CRYPTOGRAPHY

How a payment becomes private math

Three pieces make a payment both private and safe: a commitment that hides a note, a proof that spends it without revealing it, and the compliance gate that keeps the pool clean. Let us take the first two, because they are where the real cleverness lives.

When you shield, the contract does not store "you own 100 USDC." It takes the details of your note, the owner key, the token, the amount, and a secret random value, and runs them through a hash function called Poseidon. The output is your commitment, a single value like 0x8f2e34a1…4c1a91b. That commitment is added as a new leaf in an append-only tree. The whole tree then reduces to one number, the root, which is a fingerprint of every note in the pool.

A commitment is a one-way hash. You cannot run it backward to recover the owner, token, or amount.

So far, nothing has been spent. The interesting moment is the payment itself. To spend, your browser builds a zero knowledge proof. This is the heart of RAILGUN, and it is generated entirely on your device, never on a server.

A single proof quietly establishes four things at once: that you own a real note in the tree, that the note actually exists in the pool, that the amounts balance so no value is created or destroyed, and that you are not spending a note twice. The elegant part is how membership is proven. Your browser walks a secret path from your leaf up to the root. Reaching the correct root by that path is itself the proof that your note is in the tree, without ever revealing which leaf it is.

The contract never sees your note. It checks your proof against the pool's root, marks the nullifier used, and records the new notes.

A spend therefore consumes your input note, publishes a nullifier so it can never be spent again, and creates new output notes: one for the payment, one for your change. The contract verifies the proof against the current root, confirms the nullifier has not been used, and records the new commitments. No amount, no sender, and no recipient is ever revealed. Monaris's fee is simply a second output note in the very same proof, so it settles atomically and never disturbs the privacy math.

When you spend, you do not point at your note. You prove, in secret, that one of the pool's notes is yours, and that it balances. The chain checks the proof against the root and never learns which note you meant.
03 / THE INTEGRATION

Where Monaris comes in

Everything above is RAILGUN. It is powerful, and on its own it is also exactly the wall that stops normal businesses: seed phrases, gas tokens, manual syncs, raw private addresses. Monaris's contribution is not a new privacy scheme. It is a set of engineering decisions that make this specific machine usable by someone who signs in with Gmail. Two of those decisions are genuinely uncommon.

The first is to derive the private identity, not mint it. Normally a RAILGUN wallet needs its own seed phrase, one more secret for the user to store and lose. Instead, Monaris asks the user's embedded login wallet to sign one fixed message, hashes that signature into a mnemonic, and builds the RAILGUN wallet from it. Because the same login always produces the same signature, the same private wallet can be recovered from an email login. There is no seed phrase, and privacy survives a forgotten password.

The second is to run the entire engine in the browser. The prover, the Merkle trees, and the keys all live on the user's device. There is no backend wallet server holding spending power. The device builds the proof and the contract verifies it, which means even Monaris cannot spend a user's funds or forge their proofs. That is a real trust property, and it is the reason the architecture takes on so much extra work to survive inside a browser.

Add gas sponsorship to that, so the user never holds a gas token, and the fee tucked inside the proof, and the result is a payment that looks like a normal app and behaves like a private one underneath.

Contracts hold the canonical money. The browser holds the private work. Monaris earns a fee and never holds the funds.

04 / THE FLOW

One payment, end to end

Putting it together, here is what actually happens when a business pays a supplier privately. The top of each step is what the person experiences. The rest is what the system does for them, invisibly.

Four taps and a short wait on top. A full privacy pipeline underneath, none of which the user has to understand.

The whole sequence is the point of the product. A business signs in with an email and pays an invoice. Underneath, a privacy engine derives an identity, syncs a Merkle tree, runs a compliance quorum, generates a zero knowledge proof, and settles on chain with no amount or party revealed. The distance between what the user does and what the system does is the entire value.

05 / THE HONEST PART

What this is, and what it is not

It would be easy to call this anonymity. That would be the wrong word, and using it would actually be a weakness, not a strength.

Because the private wallet is derived from the login, the privacy is tied to the security of that login. The honest, defensible framing is not maximum anonymity. It is private by default with selective disclosure: payments that stay off the public ledger, but that a business can choose to reveal to an auditor when it needs to. For real commercial use, that is not a compromise. It is exactly what businesses want, because pure anonymity and real-world compliance do not coexist.

It is worth being just as clear about what remains. Keeping the keys and the prover on the device is a genuine trust property, but it is not the same as an independent audit of the contracts and the proving artifacts. The design can be excellent and still owe the last stretch to outside review and time in production. Strong cryptography, honestly described, beats strong cryptography oversold.

Monaris does not make RAILGUN more private. It makes RAILGUN's privacy something an ordinary business can actually reach.

That is the whole thesis. The shielded pool, the commitments, the proofs, and the compliance gate are RAILGUN's. The seedless identity, the in-browser engine, the sponsored gas, and the one-tap flow are how Monaris turns that machine into a product. The math stays hard. It just stops being the user's problem.

Monaris is private stablecoin payments that feel like one tap.

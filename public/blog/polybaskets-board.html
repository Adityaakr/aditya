PolyBaskets

Trade conviction, not single outcomes.

The agent native index layer for prediction markets. Built on Polymarket. Powered by Vara. This board lays out how the whole thing fits together and how the new perps feature works, so anyone on the team can see the shape of it at a glance.

The big idea

From single bets to a tradable thesis

Polymarket lets you bet on one thing at a time. Will Bitcoin hit $100K. Will this candidate win. That works beautifully for single, dated questions.

But most people do not think in single bets. They think in stories. "I am bullish on AI." "I think crypto is going up." "I think the underdogs win this weekend." PolyBaskets lets you bundle many related markets into one basket and trade that whole idea as a single position. A market becomes a bet. A basket becomes an asset.

Overview

How PolyBaskets works: three layers

There are three layers stacked on top of each other. Each one makes the layer below it more powerful. The bottom is where you group markets, the middle turns that group into a live, trackable index, and the top is where AI agents do the work for you.

The agent layer is the part a competitor cannot copy just by adding baskets to their own product. It is also where most of the long term value sits.

Architecture

What happens under the hood

This is the part that keeps the product safe and simple to build. When you go long or short a basket, PolyBaskets does not become your betting opponent. It works like a smart router. It takes your thesis, breaks it into the individual markets, and buys the right side of each one on Polymarket's existing order books. The other side of your trade is the open market, one bet at a time.

Aggregator, not the house. Through the first three phases there is no counterparty risk by design, because every position is just real bets bought on Polymarket. All of those positions are tracked together as one value, which is your NAV. And markets too thin to trade cleanly are simply not allowed into a tradeable basket. That one rule removes the biggest danger before it can ever appear.

Design choice

Three numbers, kept honest

It is tempting to call a basket an "index fund" and show one line that goes up. That would mislead people, because a prediction market is not a stock. A stock tends to drift up over time as a company grows. A basket of prediction markets has no such drift, since each market is just an odds reading between 0 and 1. So PolyBaskets shows three different numbers and never pretends one is the other.

In one line: the gauge tells you the odds, your NAV tells you your money, and the rolling index is the thing you actually trade.

The new feature

Perps on PolyBaskets: being right pays rent

Here is the heart of the new feature. A normal prediction market pays you once, on settlement day, and then it is over. Being right early earns you almost nothing while your money sits locked up waiting. The rolling index fixes that. Every time a market in your basket settles, we bank the result and roll the money into the next version of that question. Being right keeps paying, again and again, for as long as your thesis holds.

This is what gives the index a real direction. It climbs while your thesis keeps coming true and it falls when the thesis stops working. A plain average of probabilities would just wobble between 0 and 1, and a number like that is not worth trading. The rolling index is.

The new feature

Long, short, and the perp engine

Going long a basket means buying the YES side of every market inside it, weighted the way you set it. Going short means buying the NO side of every market. Both are built entirely from real Polymarket trades, so the early phases stay safe and need no special machinery. Only later, and only on the most liquid baskets, do we add a true perpetual.

Why a basket is safer to perp than a single market. A single yes or no market slams toward 0 or 1 as its deadline nears, which is exactly what breaks funding and margin in normal perp designs. A basket of many markets does not behave that way. Its value moves more smoothly, and that smoothness is precisely what a funding rate and margin need in order to work. So perping the basket is the safer version, not just the easier one to position.

Roadmap

How we build it, step by step

The plan is staged on purpose, so the risky parts come last. Each phase is useful on its own and sets up the next one. You ship most of the product before you ever touch the hard part.

Phase one is also the cheapest experiment we can run. If people build baskets, hold them, and come back to redeploy after a market settles, the whole idea is proven before we build anything complicated. If they do not do that, we learn it early and cheaply, and nothing downstream was wasted.

Why it wins

Hard to copy

The moat is not the basket idea on its own. Anyone can group markets together. The real edge is the combination of four things working as one: a router sitting on top of Polymarket's liquidity, an ecosystem of agents that build and manage baskets, a copy and compete network with real track records, and the rolling index engine running underneath it all. None of that comes free to a competitor, and none of it is the crowded race to build yet another exchange. PolyBaskets plays on a different field, the strategy and asset management layer.
